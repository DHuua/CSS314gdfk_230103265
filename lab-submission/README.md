# CS482 Lab Practicum - Bottleneck Findings

Platform: Intel Core i5-13420H (hybrid: 4 P-cores with SMT + 4 E-cores, 8 physical / 12 logical threads), L1d 48 KB, L2 1.25 MB, L3 12 MB (P-core values), 64-byte cache lines, 2x8 GB DDR5-5200 (83.2 GB/s theoretical peak), Windows 11, Python 3.13 with NumPy/Numba. Reading a 134 MB buffer costs 0.57 ns per element at stride 1 and 5.7 ns at stride 8 (about 10x), followed by a noisy plateau of 7-14 ns at larger strides, because every strided read pulls in a full 64-byte line and the prefetcher stops helping. Interchanging i-j-k to i-k-j on the salted N=1472 (M=736 block) gave a 1.57x speedup (0.49 s to 0.32 s) with identical results; the gain is moderate because the ~4.3 MB block of B fits in L3, so the strided accesses hit L3 rather than DRAM. Deliberate false sharing raised the unpadded counter runtime from 0.55 s (P=1) to 1.56 s (P=8) at constant work per process; 64-byte padding stayed at 0.52-0.55 s up to P=4 and privatization at 0.12 s, and the work-normalised Karp-Flatt metric was 0.18-0.27 for unpadded versus about 0 for padded/privatized up to P=4 (P=8 is distorted by E-cores and SMT). The 5-point Jacobi stencil has an arithmetic intensity of 0.25 FLOP/byte against a ridge point of about 3.5 FLOP/byte, so it is memory-bandwidth bound (about 2 GFLOP/s at P=1 versus a 20.8 GFLOP/s bandwidth roof). Strong scaling peaks at 2.2x on 4 processes (55% efficiency) and falls to 1.8x on 8; the fitted Amdahl serial fraction (median 0.28, S_max about 3.6) grows with P, so it reflects memory-bandwidth saturation, per-iteration barriers and hybrid cores rather than truly serial code. Weak scaling reaches a scaled speedup of 3.9 on 8 processes, well below the Gustafson prediction for the same reason.
